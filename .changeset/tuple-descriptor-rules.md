Hand-written tuple descriptors are now checked the same way at every entry point. `t.array`, `t.struct`, `t.tuple`, `t.fromOutputs` and `t.fromAbiParameter` run one canonicalizer at every nesting level. A tuple needs at least one member, every member type must be in the vocabulary, a `tuple…` member needs a well-formed tag and its own `components`, and the array-depth and static-size limits apply to every member. Before, `t.tuple`/`t.struct`/`t.array` accepted a descriptor with an unknown nested member type, a malformed nested tag such as `tuple[0]`, or an empty tuple, and `t.fromOutputs`/`t.fromAbiParameter` accepted a malformed nested tag. These now throw `TYPE_MISMATCH` at the call site, naming the member by its path. `namedArg`, `evscript` args, `s.fn` and `t.error` params and `s.tuple` apply the same limits to the canonical descriptors they accept. Before, `namedArg` and `t.error` accepted an empty tuple or arrays nested too deep inside a struct. `evscript` args and `s.tuple` reported an empty tuple as an internal error (`s.tuple` also too-deep arrays), and `s.tuple` accepted a member past the static-size cap. An `s.fn` param that was an empty tuple or a struct with arrays nested too deep failed only at compile, as an internal error, and one with a member past the static-size cap compiled. They now throw `TYPE_MISMATCH` or `UNSUPPORTED_V0` while the script records. `t.struct`, `t.tuple` and `t.array` now give an unnamed nested member `name: ''`, as `t.fromOutputs` and `t.fromAbiParameter` already did. Before, its `name` was left `undefined`, and `s.tuple`, `s.lit`, `s.fn` params and `evscript` args rejected such a descriptor with `TYPE_MISMATCH`. They now accept it, and a literal for a tuple with such a member is positional, as in abitype. `t.fromOutputs` and `t.fromAbiParameter` results are now frozen, like every other `t.*` result.
