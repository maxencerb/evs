Bound the work of decoding nested arrays whose element offsets overlap. A callee could point every offset of a `uint256[][]`, `uint8[][]` or struct array at the same element, so a few dozen KB of returndata made the decoder build hundreds of copies: hundreds of millions of gas, enough to run the whole `eth_call` out of gas even from `s.tryRead`. Inner `uint256[]`/`int256[]`/`bytes32[]` arrays now point straight into the returndata instead of being copied, so such payloads decode cheaply (`uint256[][]` decodes are also smaller and cheaper). Every other block the decoder has to build is charged the returndata bytes it mirrors — a dynamic array its length word and body, and, inside an array's elements, a struct with a dynamic member its head and a fixed-size `T[N]` of dynamic elements its offset words — to a per-call budget of the returndata size plus 8192 words, the same allowance as viem's `recursiveReadLimit`; well-formed data is never charged more than its own size, and returndata that exhausts it fails like any other malformed returndata (`success = false` under `try*` verbs, `EvsDecodeError(site)` under strict verbs). `interpret` applies the same budget. A call whose outputs hold only one charged block decoded once (a lone `string[]`, `bytes[]`, `uint256[][]` or static-struct array output, or a struct output holding exactly one such array or one normalized word array, as in `(uint64[])`) can never exhaust it — that block lies inside the returndata — so it compiles without the budget and costs no extra bytecode or gas.
